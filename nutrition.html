Nutrition and recovery

The other twenty-three hours

The hour you spend lifting breaks muscle down a little. The meals, the water, and the sleep that follow are what build it back stronger — training is the signal, and everything on this page is how your body answers it.

Nothing here asks you to count calories, cut out a food group, or buy anything. Get these basics roughly right most days and you will out-progress people with far fancier plans.

Build a plate, not a diet

Forget meal plans. At most meals, fill the plate in the proportions below and use your own hand to size each portion — it is always with you, and it grows with your body, so the portions scale without a food scale.

  • Half Vegetables and fruit

    About two fists' worth. Anything colourful counts, and frozen is as good as fresh — usually cheaper, and it never goes off in the fridge.

  • A quarter Protein

    One palm, the size and thickness of your hand. Meat, fish, eggs, tofu, beans, or Greek yogurt. Make it two palms if you are big or very hungry.

  • A quarter Carbohydrates

    A cupped handful of rice, potatoes, pasta, oats, or bread. This is what you train on — carbs are fuel, not the enemy.

  • On the side A thumb of fat

    Olive oil, nuts, cheese, avocado. You need some, and it adds up fast — which is the only reason to keep an eye on it.

How much protein you actually need

Protein is the raw material for new muscle, and it is the nutrient beginners most often fall short on. Studies of people who lift find the benefit levels off at about 1.6 grams per kilogram of bodyweight a day. Going up to 2.2 grams gives a comfortable margin; past that you are paying for nothing.

Eating around a workout

Timing matters far less than the internet suggests — what you eat across the whole day is what counts. Still, a little planning makes sessions feel noticeably better.

  1. 2–3 hours before

    A normal meal

    Carbohydrates and some protein — the plate above is ideal. Training hungry is not dangerous, but you will lift less.

  2. 30–60 minutes before

    A small top-up, if you need one

    Only if your last meal was a long time ago. Something light and easy on the stomach: a banana, a slice of toast, a yogurt.

  3. During

    Water

    Sip it between sets. A session under an hour does not need sports drinks, gels, or anything with a flavour.

  4. Afterwards

    Your next meal

    Protein and carbohydrates within a couple of hours. The famous thirty-minute "anabolic window" is really several hours wide — no need to sprint for a shaker.

Recovery is where you grow

Lifting is the stimulus; the growth happens while you rest. These habits decide how much of each session you actually get to keep.

01

Sleep

Aim for seven to nine hours. Short sleep makes every set feel heavier and slows repair, and no supplement makes up for it. A steady bedtime beats a long weekend lie-in.

02

Rest days

Three sessions a week leaves four days off, and that is on purpose. Give a muscle about 48 hours before you train it hard again, so avoid hitting the same group two days running.

03

Walking

A 20 to 30 minute walk on rest days eases soreness better than lying on the sofa, and it is the easiest win for your heart there is.

04

Water

Pale-yellow urine is the simplest guide to whether you are drinking enough. A glass with every meal and a bottle at the gym covers most people.

05

Stretching

Stretching will not prevent soreness, but a few minutes on tight spots — hips, ankles, upper back — helps you reach the positions the lifts ask for, like a deeper squat.

06

Alcohol

A drink or two will not undo your training. A heavy night will: it wrecks sleep quality and slows muscle repair, so the next session pays for it.

Food questions

Do I need to count calories?

Not to start. Build plates as above, eat protein at every meal, and weigh yourself once a week. Only start tracking if your weight has not moved the way you want after a month.

Are carbs bad for me?

No. Carbohydrates are your muscles' preferred fuel for lifting, and cutting them tends to make training miserable. Lean on whole-food sources — potatoes, rice, oats, fruit — and enjoy the rest in moderation.

Can I build muscle as a vegetarian or vegan?

Yes. Use the filter above: tofu, lentils, chickpeas, dairy, and eggs all count. Plant-based lifters do best eating a mix of sources through the day and aiming for the upper end of their protein range.

Should I eat before bed?

If you are hungry, yes. Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, or a glass of milk is an easy way to top up the day's protein, and food does not turn into fat just because it is eaten late.

What about creatine?

It is the most thoroughly researched supplement in the gym: 3–5 g of creatine monohydrate a day gives a small but real strength boost and is considered safe for healthy adults. It is still optional, and like everything else in the supplement aisle it can wait until you have trained consistently for six months. Ask a doctor first if you have any kidney condition.
