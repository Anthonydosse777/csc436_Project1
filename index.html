Your first eight weeks under the bar

Sixteen exercises cover nearly every muscle worth training when you are starting out. Learn them, show up three times a week, and add a little weight whenever the last rep stops feeling hard. That is the entire method — everything else is detail you can pick up once you are already training.

The movements below are grouped the way most beginner programmes split them: push, pull, legs, and core. Pick one exercise from each group, do three sets of eight to twelve reps, and rest about ninety seconds between sets.

Push

Anything where you move weight away from your body. These train the chest, the front of the shoulders, and the triceps — the muscles that do the work when you push a heavy door open.

Push-Up

Muscles
Chest, triceps
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Set your hands slightly wider than your shoulders, body in one straight line from head to heels.
  2. Lower until your chest is a fist's height from the floor, elbows angled back at about 45 degrees.
  3. Press back up without letting your hips sag or pike upward.

Bench Press

Muscles
Chest, shoulders, triceps
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Pin your shoulder blades back against the bench and keep both feet flat on the floor.
  2. Lower the bar under control to the middle of your chest, wrists stacked over your elbows.
  3. Drive the bar back up. Use a spotter or the safety pins any time you train near your limit.

Shoulder Press

Muscles
Shoulders, triceps
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Start with the dumbbells at ear height, palms facing forward.
  2. Press straight overhead until your arms lock out, keeping your ribs down.
  3. If your lower back arches to help, the weight is too heavy — drop it and press again.

Tricep Pushdown

Muscles
Triceps
Difficulty
Intermediate
  1. Set the cable high and grip the rope with your elbows tucked against your ribs.
  2. Push down until your arms lock out, keeping your upper arms completely still.
  3. Let the rope rise back slowly. If your shoulders roll forward, drop the weight.

Pull

Anything where you draw weight toward your body. These build the back and biceps, and they are the counterweight that keeps all that pushing from rounding your shoulders forward.

Lat Pulldown

Muscles
Lats, biceps
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Set the thigh pad so your legs stay anchored, and grip the bar wider than your shoulders.
  2. Pull the bar to your collarbone by driving your elbows down, not by yanking with your hands.
  3. Let the bar rise all the way back up so your lats get a full stretch each rep.

Cable Row

Muscles
Mid back, biceps
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Set a mid-height cable, brace your feet, and keep a slight bend in your knees.
  2. Draw the handle to your waist, squeezing your shoulder blades together at the end.
  3. Return slowly. Do not let your torso rock back and forth to move the weight.

Pull-Up

Muscles
Lats, biceps, forearms
Difficulty
Advanced
  1. Hang from the bar with your hands just outside shoulder width and your shoulders engaged.
  2. Pull until your chin clears the bar, keeping your legs still rather than kicking.
  3. Lower all the way down. Use a resistance band or the assisted machine to build up to it.

Bicep Curl

Muscles
Biceps
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Stand with a dumbbell in each hand, palms facing forward and elbows tucked to your ribs.
  2. Curl the weight up without letting your elbows drift ahead of your body.
  3. Lower slowly — the way down builds as much as the way up.

Legs

The largest muscles you own, and the ones beginners skip most often. Training them is what makes everything else — stairs, carrying groceries, standing up — quietly easier.

Goblet Squat

Muscles
Quads, glutes
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Hold a single dumbbell against your chest with both hands, feet about shoulder width.
  2. Sit straight down between your knees, keeping your heels flat and your chest tall.
  3. Stand back up by driving through the middle of your foot.

Leg Press

Muscles
Quads, glutes
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Place your feet shoulder width apart in the middle of the platform.
  2. Lower until your knees reach about 90 degrees, never letting your lower back peel off the pad.
  3. Press back out without snapping your knees straight at the top.

Walking Lunge

Muscles
Quads, glutes, hamstrings
Difficulty
Intermediate
  1. Hold a dumbbell in each hand and take a long step forward.
  2. Drop your back knee toward the floor, keeping your torso upright the whole way.
  3. Push off the front foot into your next step. Start with bodyweight until your balance settles.

Calf Raise

Muscles
Calves
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Stand with the balls of your feet on a step and your heels hanging off the edge.
  2. Drop your heels for a full stretch, then rise as high onto your toes as you can.
  3. Pause at the top for a full second. Rushing this one wastes the set.

Core

Your midsection's real job is resisting movement, not producing it. These four train it to stay braced — which is what protects your back under every other lift on this page.

Plank

Muscles
Whole core
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Set your forearms on the floor directly under your shoulders.
  2. Squeeze your glutes and abs so your body holds one straight line.
  3. Hold for thirty honest seconds. That beats two sagging minutes every time.

Crunch

Muscles
Upper abs
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet flat.
  2. Curl your shoulders off the floor using your abs, not by pulling on your neck.
  3. Lower under control rather than dropping back down.

Russian Twist

Muscles
Obliques
Difficulty
Intermediate
  1. Sit with your knees bent and lean back until you feel your abs switch on.
  2. Rotate a weight from one hip to the other, turning through your ribs rather than your arms.
  3. Keep your chest lifted. The moment your back rounds, stop the set.

Decline Sit-Up

Muscles
Abs, hip flexors
Difficulty
Intermediate
  1. Hook your feet under the pads of a decline bench and cross your arms at your chest.
  2. Curl up using your abs rather than swinging your arms for momentum.
  3. Lower under control. Start on a shallow decline and increase the angle over time.

Questions everyone has

Is everyone watching me?

No. They are looking at their phone between sets, exactly like you will be. The gym is the least observant room you will be in all week.

How do I know the weight is right?

The last two reps should be genuinely hard, but your technique should not change. If the final rep looks like the first, go heavier. If your back rounds at rep eight, go lighter.

How sore should I be?

A dull ache for a day or two is normal, especially early on. Sharp pain, joint pain, or soreness lasting past four days means you did too much too soon.

Do I need protein powder?

No. It is a convenient way to reach a protein target and nothing more — ordinary food does the same job. Skip every other supplement until you have trained consistently for six months.

What if I miss a week?

Come back and repeat your last session at the same weight. You lose far less than you think, and picking it back up is not the same as starting over.

Know Your Muscles

"Train shoulders" is not one job — it is three. Every group below splits into parts that sit in different places and respond to different angles, which is why two people doing "chest day" can end up with completely different results. Open a group to see its parts, where each one sits, and the lifts that hit it hardest.

Shoulders

3 parts

Your shoulder — the deltoid — is one muscle with three heads pointing in three directions. Training only the front one is the most common beginner mistake, because every press already trains it for you.

Front Delt

The front of your shoulder. It does most of the work in pressing movements — anything you push overhead or away from your chest.

Trained by

  • Overhead Dumbbell Press
  • Front Raise
  • Incline Bench Press

Side Delt

The cap on the outside of your shoulder. It lifts your arm out to the side, and it is the part that makes shoulders look wide.

Trained by

  • Dumbbell Lateral Raise
  • Cable Lateral Raise
  • Upright Row

Rear Delt

The back of your shoulder. It pulls your arm backwards and helps hold your shoulders back rather than rounded forward over a desk.

Trained by

  • Face Pull
  • Reverse Pec Deck
  • Bent-Over Reverse Fly

Chest

3 parts

The chest is really one fan of muscle, the pectoralis major. Splitting it into three is a gym convention rather than three separate muscles — but the angle you press at genuinely changes which fibres do most of the work.

Upper Chest

The top of the chest, just below your collarbone. It takes over whenever you press on an upward angle, and it is the part most beginners under-train.

Trained by

  • Incline Bench Press
  • Incline Dumbbell Press
  • Low-to-High Cable Fly

Mid Chest

The thick middle section, and the bulk of the muscle. It is the main mover on any flat press or push-up.

Trained by

  • Flat Bench Press
  • Push-Up
  • Machine Chest Press

Lower Chest

The bottom edge of the chest, where it meets your ribs. It works hardest when you press on a downward angle or lean forward on a dip.

Trained by

  • Decline Bench Press
  • Chest Dip
  • High-to-Low Cable Fly

Back

3 parts

Your back is several separate muscles stacked on top of each other. These three cover almost everything a beginner needs to think about.

Lats

The big fan-shaped muscles down the sides of your back. They pull your arms down and in, which is why they run every pull-up and every pulldown.

Trained by

  • Lat Pulldown
  • Pull-Up
  • Straight-Arm Pulldown

Traps

The diamond of muscle between your neck and your shoulder blades. It shrugs your shoulders up and pins your shoulder blades together.

Trained by

  • Dumbbell Shrug
  • Face Pull
  • Barbell Row

Lower Back

The column of muscle running either side of your spine above your hips. Its whole job is keeping your back straight while everything else lifts.

Trained by

  • Back Extension
  • Romanian Deadlift
  • Bird Dog

Biceps

2 parts

"Biceps" literally means two heads. They share one tendon at the elbow, so both work on every curl — where your elbows sit just decides which one leads.

Long Head

The outer half of the biceps — the part that makes the peak when you flex. It is stretched hardest when your elbows sit behind your body.

Trained by

  • Incline Dumbbell Curl
  • Drag Curl
  • Narrow-Grip Barbell Curl

Short Head

The inner half, closer to your chest. It adds the width you see when you look at your arm straight on, and it leads when your elbows are out in front of you.

Trained by

  • Preacher Curl
  • Wide-Grip Barbell Curl
  • Concentration Curl

Triceps

3 parts

Roughly two-thirds of your upper arm is triceps, not biceps. It has three heads, and only one of them crosses the shoulder — which is why overhead work feels so different.

Long Head

The inner head, and the biggest of the three. It runs all the way up to your shoulder blade, so it only gets a full stretch when your arm is overhead.

Trained by

  • Overhead Cable Extension
  • Skull Crusher
  • Overhead Dumbbell Extension

Lateral Head

The outer head, on the thumb side of the back of your arm. It is the one you see from the side, and it takes over on heavy pushdowns.

Trained by

  • Rope Pushdown
  • Close-Grip Bench Press
  • Bench Dip

Medial Head

The small deep head sitting just above your elbow. It helps on every tricep exercise and does its most work in the last few inches of a lockout.

Trained by

  • Reverse-Grip Pushdown
  • Close-Grip Bench Press
  • Parallel Bar Dip

Legs

4 parts

Four groups cover the whole leg: the front of the thigh, the back of the thigh, the hips, and the lower leg. Squats alone do not cover all four.

Quads

The four muscles on the front of your thigh. They straighten your knee, so they run every squat, every step up, and every flight of stairs.

Trained by

  • Goblet Squat
  • Leg Press
  • Leg Extension

Hamstrings

The back of your thigh. They bend your knee and control your hips as you fold forward — and they are the muscle most likely to pull if you never train them.

Trained by

  • Romanian Deadlift
  • Lying Leg Curl
  • Good Morning

Glutes

Your backside, and the strongest muscle group you own. It drives your hips forward out of the bottom of a squat, a jump, or a sprint.

Trained by

  • Hip Thrust
  • Bulgarian Split Squat
  • Glute Bridge

Calves

The two muscles on the back of your lower leg. They point your toes down, which is every step, every jump, and every push off the floor.

Trained by

  • Standing Calf Raise
  • Seated Calf Raise
  • Leg Press Calf Raise

Core

3 parts

Your abs are one long sheet of muscle, not separate blocks — you cannot contract half of it on its own. What the exercises below do is bias the work toward the top, the bottom, or the sides.

Upper Abs

The top of the sheet, between your ribs and your navel. It does the most work when you curl your shoulders toward your hips.

Trained by

  • Crunch
  • Cable Crunch
  • Decline Sit-Up

Lower Abs

The bottom of the same sheet, below your navel. It does the most work when you bring your hips and legs toward your chest instead of the other way round.

Trained by

  • Hanging Leg Raise
  • Reverse Crunch
  • Lying Leg Raise

Obliques

The muscles down the sides of your waist. They twist your torso, and — more often — stop it twisting when something tries to pull you sideways.

Trained by

  • Russian Twist
  • Side Plank
  • Cable Woodchop
