Beginner reference

Learn the lifts before you chase the weight.

Sixteen exercises, split the way most beginner programs are written: push, pull, legs and core. Every card tells you which muscles the lift works, how hard it is to learn, and the two or three cues that matter most. Start light, move well, add weight later.

Push

Chest, shoulders and triceps — anything where you press the weight away from you.

Bench Press

Chest, front delts, triceps Intermediate
  1. Lie flat, feet planted, and grip the bar a little wider than your shoulders.
  2. Lower the bar slowly to the middle of your chest, keeping your elbows tucked at about 45 degrees.
  3. Press back up until your arms are straight, without bouncing the bar off your chest.

Shoulder Press

Shoulders, triceps Intermediate
  1. Stand with the bar or dumbbells at shoulder height and your feet under your hips.
  2. Squeeze your glutes so your lower back does not arch, then press straight overhead.
  3. Lower under control until your hands are back at your shoulders.

Push-Up

Chest, triceps, core Beginner
  1. Set your hands under your shoulders and squeeze your body into one straight line.
  2. Lower your chest to just above the floor with your elbows angled back, not flared out.
  3. Push the floor away and finish with your shoulder blades spread wide.

Tricep Dip

Triceps, lower chest Intermediate
  1. Support yourself on parallel bars or a bench with your arms locked straight.
  2. Bend your elbows and lower until your upper arms are about parallel to the floor.
  3. Press back to the top, keeping your shoulders down and away from your ears.

Pull

Back and biceps — anything where you drag the weight toward you or pull yourself up.

Lat Pulldown

Lats, upper back, biceps Beginner
  1. Sit with your thighs locked under the pad and take a wide overhand grip.
  2. Pull the bar to your collarbone by driving your elbows down, not by leaning back.
  3. Let the bar rise slowly until your arms are straight again.

Seated Row

Mid back, rear delts, biceps Beginner
  1. Sit tall with a small bend in your knees and your feet flat on the plate.
  2. Pull the handle to your belly button and squeeze your shoulder blades together.
  3. Return slowly and keep your back straight instead of rocking back and forth.

Bicep Curl

Biceps, forearms Beginner
  1. Stand with a dumbbell in each hand and your elbows pinned to your sides.
  2. Curl the weight up by bending only at the elbow — your upper arm stays still.
  3. Lower all the way down, slower than you lifted it.

Pull-Up

Lats, upper back, biceps Intermediate
  1. Hang from the bar with an overhand grip and your arms fully straight.
  2. Pull until your chin clears the bar, keeping your ribs down and legs quiet.
  3. Lower under control. Use a band or the assisted machine until you can do five clean reps.

Legs

Quads, hamstrings, glutes and calves — the biggest muscles you own, so train them first when you are fresh.

Back Squat

Quads, glutes, core Intermediate
  1. Rest the bar across your upper back and stand with your feet about shoulder-width apart.
  2. Sit down and back, keeping your chest up and your knees tracking over your toes.
  3. Stand up by pushing through your whole foot, driving your heels into the floor.

Walking Lunge

Quads, glutes, balance Beginner
  1. Take a long step forward with a dumbbell in each hand.
  2. Drop your back knee toward the floor until both knees are bent about 90 degrees.
  3. Push through the front heel to stand, then step through with the other leg.

Leg Press

Quads, glutes, hamstrings Beginner
  1. Sit back in the seat and place your feet shoulder-width apart on the platform.
  2. Lower the sled until your knees reach about 90 degrees, keeping your hips on the seat.
  3. Press back up without snapping your knees straight at the top.

Calf Raise

Calves Beginner
  1. Stand with the balls of your feet on a step and your heels hanging off the edge.
  2. Rise as high onto your toes as you can and hold for a second at the top.
  3. Lower your heels below the step for a slow stretch before the next rep.

Core

Abs, obliques and lower back — trained to resist movement, not just to crunch.

Plank

Abs, lower back, shoulders Beginner
  1. Rest on your forearms and toes with your elbows under your shoulders.
  2. Squeeze your glutes and abs so your hips do not sag or pike up.
  3. Hold for 20 to 40 seconds while breathing normally.

Crunch

Upper abs Beginner
  1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet flat on the floor.
  2. Curl your shoulders a few inches off the ground — your lower back stays down.
  3. Lower slowly and rest your hands lightly at your temples, never pulling on your neck.

Lying Leg Raise

Lower abs, hip flexors Intermediate
  1. Lie flat with your hands under your hips for support.
  2. Lift both legs, close to straight, until they point at the ceiling.
  3. Lower them slowly and stop before your lower back peels off the floor.

Russian Twist

Obliques, abs Intermediate
  1. Sit with your knees bent and lean back until you feel your abs switch on.
  2. Hold a plate or ball at your chest and rotate it to one hip, then the other.
  3. Turn from your ribs rather than swinging your arms, and keep your chest tall.

Beginner questions

The five things people ask in their first month.

How many days a week should I train?

Three is plenty to start. A push day, a pull day and a leg day, with core added to any two of them, covers your whole body with a rest day in between each session.

How much weight should I use?

Pick a weight you could lift about ten times, and stop two reps short of failing. If your form breaks down before the last rep, the weight is too heavy — that is a form problem, not a toughness problem.

How many sets and reps?

Three sets of eight to twelve reps works for almost every exercise here. Rest 60 to 90 seconds between sets, and closer to two minutes on squats and presses.

Is soreness a sign that it worked?

Not really. Soreness mostly tells you that a movement was new to you. Progress is better measured by adding a rep or a small amount of weight from week to week.

Do I need to warm up?

Yes. Five minutes on a bike or rower, then two light sets of the first exercise. It takes almost no time and it makes the working sets feel much better.
