Your first eight weeks under the bar

Sixteen exercises cover nearly every muscle worth training when you are starting out. Learn them, show up three times a week, and add a little weight whenever the last rep stops feeling hard. That is the entire method — everything else is detail you can pick up once you are already training.

The movements below are grouped the way most beginner programmes split them: push, pull, legs, and core. Pick one exercise from each group, do three sets of eight to twelve reps, and rest about ninety seconds between sets.

Your experience level

All sixteen exercises, with the plain steps for each one.

Push

Anything where you move weight away from your body. These train the chest, the front of the shoulders, and the triceps — the muscles that do the work when you push a heavy door open.

Push-Up

Muscles
Chest, triceps
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Set your hands slightly wider than your shoulders, body in one straight line from head to heels.
  2. Lower until your chest is a fist's height from the floor, elbows angled back at about 45 degrees.
  3. Press back up without letting your hips sag or pike upward.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Once fifteen clean reps feel easy, do not just add reps — slow the lowering to a three-count and pause an inch off the floor.
  • Advanced Add load: feet on a bench, or a plate on your upper back, going up in the smallest increment you have. Deficit push-ups off dumbbells buy you extra range.

Bench Press

Muscles
Chest, shoulders, triceps
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Pin your shoulder blades back against the bench and keep both feet flat on the floor.
  2. Lower the bar under control to the middle of your chest, wrists stacked over your elbows.
  3. Drive the bar back up. Use a spotter or the safety pins any time you train near your limit.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Progressive overload: add the smallest plates you have — 2.5 kg total — only after you hit the top of your rep range on every set.
  • Advanced Pause a full second on the chest to kill the bounce, or run a heavy top set of three to five reps and then a back-off set of eight to ten.

Shoulder Press

Muscles
Shoulders, triceps
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Start with the dumbbells at ear height, palms facing forward.
  2. Press straight overhead until your arms lock out, keeping your ribs down.
  3. If your lower back arches to help, the weight is too heavy — drop it and press again.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Overload here is slow. Expect to sit on the same dumbbells for weeks, and jump by one to two kilos when you do move.
  • Advanced Do not grind these to failure — the shoulder joint pays for it. Stop a rep short and add a set instead of chasing the last one.

Tricep Pushdown

Muscles
Triceps
Difficulty
Intermediate
  1. Set the cable high and grip the rope with your elbows tucked against your ribs.
  2. Push down until your arms lock out, keeping your upper arms completely still.
  3. Let the rope rise back slowly. If your shoulders roll forward, drop the weight.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate This is an accessory, not a main lift. Earn a hard squeeze and a full lockout at your current weight before you touch the pin.
  • Advanced Do not overload it. If the stack is lifting you onto your toes you are rowing, not extending — use slow negatives or a drop set instead.

Pull

Anything where you draw weight toward your body. These build the back and biceps, and they are the counterweight that keeps all that pushing from rounding your shoulders forward.

Lat Pulldown

Muscles
Lats, biceps
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Set the thigh pad so your legs stay anchored, and grip the bar wider than your shoulders.
  2. Pull the bar to your collarbone by driving your elbows down, not by yanking with your hands.
  3. Let the bar rise all the way back up so your lats get a full stretch each rep.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Progressive overload: one plate on the stack, or hold the bar at your collarbone for a full second on every rep.
  • Advanced Treat this as volume alongside pull-ups rather than a replacement. Heavy near-max pulldowns teach you very little.

Cable Row

Muscles
Mid back, biceps
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Set a mid-height cable, brace your feet, and keep a slight bend in your knees.
  2. Draw the handle to your waist, squeezing your shoulder blades together at the end.
  3. Return slowly. Do not let your torso rock back and forth to move the weight.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Add weight only while your torso stays still. The moment you start rocking to move the handle, drop back down a plate.
  • Advanced Single-arm rows expose the weaker side. Do that side first and match the strong side to whatever it managed.

Pull-Up

Muscles
Lats, biceps, forearms
Difficulty
Advanced
  1. Hang from the bar with your hands just outside shoulder width and your shoulders engaged.
  2. Pull until your chin clears the bar, keeping your legs still rather than kicking.
  3. Lower all the way down. Use a resistance band or the assisted machine to build up to it.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Cannot do one yet? Use a band or the assisted machine and take the assistance down a notch every week or two.
  • Advanced Progressive overload: once you clear ten clean reps, add load with a dip belt or a dumbbell between your feet, a couple of kilos at a time.

Bicep Curl

Muscles
Biceps
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Stand with a dumbbell in each hand, palms facing forward and elbows tucked to your ribs.
  2. Curl the weight up without letting your elbows drift ahead of your body.
  3. Lower slowly — the way down builds as much as the way up.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Small muscle, small jumps. Go up one to two kilos, and only while your elbows stay pinned to your sides.
  • Advanced Do not ego-load these — swinging hands the work to your back. Slow the negative, or curl lying back on an incline bench for a full stretch.

Legs

The largest muscles you own, and the ones beginners skip most often. Training them is what makes everything else — stairs, carrying groceries, standing up — quietly easier.

Goblet Squat

Muscles
Quads, glutes
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Hold a single dumbbell against your chest with both hands, feet about shoulder width.
  2. Sit straight down between your knees, keeping your heels flat and your chest tall.
  3. Stand back up by driving through the middle of your foot.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Progressive overload: a heavier bell, or a two-second pause at the bottom of every rep before you stand up.
  • Advanced This one has a ceiling — you run out of dumbbell long before you run out of legs. Move to a barbell front or back squat.

Leg Press

Muscles
Quads, glutes
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Place your feet shoulder width apart in the middle of the platform.
  2. Lower until your knees reach about 90 degrees, never letting your lower back peel off the pad.
  3. Press back out without snapping your knees straight at the top.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Progressive overload: add a plate per side once the last rep still feels like you had two more in you.
  • Advanced Easy to pile plates on with half a rep. Keep the depth honest — hips planted, lower back flat — before the weight goes up.

Walking Lunge

Muscles
Quads, glutes, hamstrings
Difficulty
Intermediate
  1. Hold a dumbbell in each hand and take a long step forward.
  2. Drop your back knee toward the floor, keeping your torso upright the whole way.
  3. Push off the front foot into your next step. Start with bodyweight until your balance settles.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Progressive overload: add steps before you add weight, then carry dumbbells at your sides.
  • Advanced Balance caps the load here, not your legs. Bulgarian split squats or a deficit lunge hit the same muscles far harder.

Calf Raise

Muscles
Calves
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Stand with the balls of your feet on a step and your heels hanging off the edge.
  2. Drop your heels for a full stretch, then rise as high onto your toes as you can.
  3. Pause at the top for a full second. Rushing this one wastes the set.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Calves want reps — fifteen to twenty a set, with a full pause stretched at the bottom of each one.
  • Advanced Range beats load. Two seconds down, one second squeezed at the top, and stop bouncing off the bottom.

Core

Your midsection's real job is resisting movement, not producing it. These four train it to stay braced — which is what protects your back under every other lift on this page.

Plank

Muscles
Whole core
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Set your forearms on the floor directly under your shoulders.
  2. Squeeze your glutes and abs so your body holds one straight line.
  3. Hold for thirty honest seconds. That beats two sagging minutes every time.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate More time is not overload — past a minute you are only getting uncomfortable. Reach one arm out in front of you instead.
  • Advanced Swap to harder anti-movement work: long-lever planks, ab wheel rollouts, or dead bugs holding a light weight.

Crunch

Muscles
Upper abs
Difficulty
Beginner
  1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet flat.
  2. Curl your shoulders off the floor using your abs, not by pulling on your neck.
  3. Lower under control rather than dropping back down.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Slow the movement before you add reps. Ten controlled crunches do more than twenty fast ones.
  • Advanced Diminishing returns at bodyweight. Cable crunches or a weighted decline let you actually load the abs and progress them.

Russian Twist

Muscles
Obliques
Difficulty
Intermediate
  1. Sit with your knees bent and lean back until you feel your abs switch on.
  2. Rotate a weight from one hip to the other, turning through your ribs rather than your arms.
  3. Keep your chest lifted. The moment your back rounds, stop the set.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Progressive overload: slow each rotation to a two-count and pause at each hip before you reach for a heavier ball.
  • Advanced Do not load these heavily — the spare force goes into your lower back. Cable or band rotations give you the same job with less cost.

Decline Sit-Up

Muscles
Abs, hip flexors
Difficulty
Intermediate
  1. Hook your feet under the pads of a decline bench and cross your arms at your chest.
  2. Curl up using your abs rather than swinging your arms for momentum.
  3. Lower under control. Start on a shallow decline and increase the angle over time.

Taking it further

  • Intermediate Progressive overload: a steeper decline first, then hold a plate against your chest.
  • Advanced Hold the plate overhead rather than at your chest — it lengthens the lever without stacking as much load on your spine.

Questions everyone has

Is everyone watching me?

No. They are looking at their phone between sets, exactly like you will be. The gym is the least observant room you will be in all week.

How do I know the weight is right?

The last two reps should be genuinely hard, but your technique should not change. If the final rep looks like the first, go heavier. If your back rounds at rep eight, go lighter.

How sore should I be?

A dull ache for a day or two is normal, especially early on. Sharp pain, joint pain, or soreness lasting past four days means you did too much too soon.

Do I need protein powder?

No. It is a convenient way to reach a protein target and nothing more — ordinary food does the same job. Skip every other supplement until you have trained consistently for six months. The nutrition page works out how much protein you actually need.

What if I miss a week?

Come back and repeat your last session at the same weight. You lose far less than you think, and picking it back up is not the same as starting over.
